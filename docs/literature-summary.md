Background Literature Review: AI-Assisted Variant Interpretation, Explainable AI, and Workflow Provenance

GlassBox AI Triage After nf-core/sarek —Systems Medicine
The following review summarises existing research across three domains central to the GlassBox AI Triage project: AI-assisted interpretation of genomic variants, explainable ("glass-box") AI for clinical genomics, and workflow provenance tracking. Each body of work demonstrates the maturity of its respective field, and together they reveal a gap that the project addresses, an affordable, auditable, reproducible triage layer that unifies all three capabilities inside a single lightweight Nextflow subworkflow.

1. AI-Assisted Interpretation of Genomic Variants
Ioannidis et al. introduced REVEL (Rare Exome Variant Ensemble Learner), a random-forest ensemble combining thirteen individual pathogenicity prediction tools, including SIFT, PolyPhen-2, FATHMM, VEST, MutPred, and eight conservation scores, to score missense variants on a 0–1 scale.1 Trained on recently discovered disease variants and rare neutral exome variants, REVEL outperformed all seven competing ensemble methods (MetaSVM, MetaLR, CADD, DANN, Condel, KGGSeq, and Eigen) across two independent test sets, achieving AUC improvements of 0.027–0.143 over the next-best method.1 Its strongest advantage was on rare variants with allele frequencies below 0.5%, precisely the category most commonly encountered in NGS-based pipelines such as nf-core/sarek.1 Pre-computed scores for all possible human missense variants are publicly available, making REVEL a practical foundation for any post-pipeline triage step.1
Cheng et al. published AlphaMissense, a deep learning model fine-tuned from AlphaFold on human and primate population-frequency data, producing pathogenicity scores for all 71 million possible single amino-acid substitutions in the human proteome.2 By integrating structural context, protein language modelling, and weak labels derived from population frequency rather than human-curated annotations, AlphaMissense avoids the circularity biases that affect tools trained directly on clinical databases.2 It achieved an auROC of 0.940 on a class-balanced ClinVar test set, surpassing the prior best non-ClinVar-trained model (EVE, auROC 0.911), and confidently classified 89% of all missense variants as either likely pathogenic or likely benign at 90% precision.2 The scale and accessibility of these pre-computed predictions make them directly relevant to any triage layer that must rank sarek VCF output without GPU compute.2
De Paoli et al. approached variant interpretation differently with VarChat, a generative AI tool that retrieves and summarises the published literature associated with a queried variant, returning a concise clinical summary with ranked PubMed references and linked ClinVar records.3 VarChat demonstrates that even where computational scores exist, clinicians need natural-language synthesis to act on them efficiently.3 The paper also documents the risk of "hallucination" in LLM-based systems and stresses that AI outputs must be clearly grounded in verifiable, citable sources3, a transparency concern directly addressed by the audit-bundle architecture of our project.

2. Explainable AI ("Glass-Box" AI) for Clinical Genomics
Abe et al. proposed an XAI framework for variant pathogenicity estimation that couples a high-performance deep-learning engine (Deep Tensor) with a large-scale knowledge graph integrating ClinVar, dbNSFP, COSMIC, and dbscSNV, totalling over 15 billion RDF triples.4 Deep Tensor achieved an AUC of 0.98, outperforming both random forests (AUC 0.97) and decision trees (AUC 0.90), while simultaneously generating natural-language explanatory sentences structured around ACMG classification criteria.4 Each sentence corresponds to a distinct, independently verifiable evidence item (e.g., a PROVEAN score, a neighbouring pathogenic variant in ClinVar, or a COSMIC entry), rather than requiring all conditions to be satisfied simultaneously as in a decision tree.4 The authors identify two core XAI challenges directly relevant to our project: combining high accuracy with genuine interpretability, and ensuring explanations are legible to clinicians rather than to AI developers.4 Our glass-box design addresses both: triage outputs must carry sufficient metadata, model version, prompt version, container digest, decision rationale, so that any reviewer can audit and, if necessary, challenge individual decisions independently.

3. Workflow Provenance Tracking
Leo et al. presented Workflow Run RO-Crate (WRROC), an extension of the RO-Crate packaging standard that captures both prospective provenance (the workflow plan) and retrospective provenance (what actually happened during execution) at configurable levels of granularity.5 Three nested profiles serve different use cases: Process Run Crate for individual tool executions, Workflow Run Crate for complete managed workflow runs, and Provenance Run Crate for fine-grained step-level detail.5 At the time of publication, WRROC had been implemented in six workflow management systems, Galaxy, COMPSs, StreamFlow, WfExS, Sapporo, and Autosubmit, demonstrating broad applicability across heterogeneous environments.5 The authors demonstrated that WRROC-packaged provenance is sufficient to automatically re-execute a workflow from its archived record: the same digital pathology CWL workflow was successfully reproduced from provenance records produced by two different execution engines (cwltool via runcrate and StreamFlow), yielding fully interoperable outputs.5 The SPARQL-queryable RDF representation additionally allows downstream users to programmatically trace any output back to the specific tool version and input that produced it.5
Our audit-bundle specification, logging model version, prompt version, container digest, sarek commit, input hash, and run timestamp, implements a lightweight, Nextflow-native subset of the WRROC philosophy,5 adapted for teams that cannot deploy full RO-Crate infrastructure but still require that every AI triage decision be reconstructable and auditable.
Where Our Project Fits
Each body of literature reviewed here addresses one dimension of the problem that GlassBox AI Triage tackles as a whole. REVEL1 and AlphaMissense2 establish that accurate, scalable AI-assisted variant prioritisation is technically mature. Abe et al.4 demonstrate that high accuracy and genuine interpretability are not mutually exclusive. And WRROC5 demonstrates that computational outputs can be packaged with sufficient metadata to be fully auditable and reproducible. What is absent from the existing literature is a cost-zero-by-default implementation that combines all three, an auditable, schema-validated AI triage step embedded natively in a Nextflow subworkflow that small labs can run on GitHub Codespaces without GPU infrastructure or cloud budget. The GlassBox AI Triage project addresses precisely this gap.
